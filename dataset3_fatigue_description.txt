================================================================================
DATASET — WEARABLE FATIGUE MONITOR FOR SHIFT WORKERS
Class Challenge
================================================================================

OVERVIEW
--------
Fifteen factory workers wore wristband sensors for 14 days across rotating
day and night shifts. The bands recorded heart rate, heart rate variability
(HRV), skin temperature, step count per 5-minute window, and galvanic skin
response (a measure of physiological stress) continuously. Workers
self-reported a fatigue score (1 to 5) at the start and end of each shift.
Occupational health guidelines define fatigue scores of 4 or above during
active shifts as a safety risk requiring intervention.

FILE
----
worker_fatigue_data.csv

ROWS
----
60,480 rows (15 workers x 4,032 timestamps per worker)

COLUMNS
-------
timestamp            Date and time of reading (5-min intervals)
worker_id            Worker identifier (W01 to W15)
night_shift          1 = night shift (18:00-06:00), 0 = day shift (06:00-18:00)
shift_hour           Hours elapsed since the current shift started (0-12)
heart_rate_bpm       Heart rate in beats per minute
hrv_ms               Heart rate variability in milliseconds (higher = less stressed)
skin_temp_c          Skin surface temperature (Celsius)
steps_5min           Step count in the 5-minute recording window
gsr_microsiemens     Galvanic skin response (higher = more physiological stress)
fatigue_score        TARGET (regression) — self-reported fatigue 1=alert, 5=exhausted
dangerous_fatigue    TARGET (classification) — 1 = fatigue score >= 4 during active shift

KEY STATISTICS
--------------
Number of workers:       15
Collection period:       14 days
Temporal resolution:     5 minutes
Dangerous fatigue rate:  ~18% of readings
Night shift proportion:  ~50% of active shift readings
Missing values:          ~3% across heart_rate_bpm, hrv_ms,
                         skin_temp_c, and gsr_microsiemens

PHYSIOLOGICAL CONTEXT
---------------------
Heart rate:     Rises with physical activity and increases further with
                cumulative fatigue across a long shift.
HRV:            Decreases as fatigue increases. Low HRV is associated
                with impaired decision-making and slower reaction times.
Skin temperature: Rises slightly with sustained exertion and stress.
GSR:            Rises with both physical exertion and psychological stress.
                Night shift workers show consistently higher GSR.
Shift hour:     A derived temporal feature included in the dataset.
                Fatigue increases non-linearly with hours worked.

NOTES
------------------
- Two targets are provided. Use fatigue_score for regression.
  Use dangerous_fatigue for classification.
- The dataset is structured as a time series per worker. For tabular
  models (XGBoost, Random Forest) treat each row independently.
  For LSTM, consider building 30-minute sliding windows per worker.
- Night shift workers show higher fatigue scores for the same number
  of hours worked. night_shift is an important feature.
- shift_hour is already engineered for you. Consider whether adding
  shift_hour^2 (a polynomial feature) captures the non-linear fatigue curve.

RESEARCH QUESTIONS
================================================================================

PRIMARY QUESTION
----------------
Q1. Can we predict a worker's fatigue score from physiological wearable
    sensor data — and which sensor gives the earliest warning of high fatigue?

    Suggested approach: Train an XGBoost or Random Forest regressor to
    predict fatigue_score. Evaluate with MAE and R2. Use feature importance
    to identify the leading physiological indicator of fatigue onset.

SECONDARY QUESTION
------------------
Q2. Can we automatically flag dangerous fatigue (score >= 4 during an active
    shift) before the worker self-reports it — using only sensor readings
    from earlier in the shift?

    Suggested approach: Train an Isolation Forest on sensor readings from
    rested workers (fatigue_score = 1 or 2). Score all readings and compare
    the anomaly score distribution between rested and fatigued workers.
    Alternatively, train a binary classifier using dangerous_fatigue as the
    target and evaluate recall for the dangerous class. You can also exoplore Build a 30-minute sliding window LSTM per worker and classify
    dangerous_fatigue from the sequence of physiological readings.
    Compare its recall against Isolation Forest.
